The moment we became our own case study

A few weeks ago we did the thing every business should do but almost none actually do: we asked the AI engines what they say about us. We typed "what is Specularis" into Perplexity.

It told us we were lapis specularis — a translucent mineral the Romans used as window glass before they figured out how to make glass.

Not the company. The rock.

Why did AI confuse us with a mineral?

Here's the uncomfortable part: the AI wasn't being dumb. It was doing its job with the information it had.

When you ask an AI engine "what is X," it doesn't invent an answer. It resolves the name against what the web has consistently established. The mineral has a Wikipedia page, a knowledge-graph entry, and centuries of consistent references. Specularis-the-company had a website. That was it. No knowledge-graph entity, almost no mentions from anywhere except our own site.

Put a well-documented mineral next to a company with a single website, and AI picks the mineral every time. That's not a glitch. That's entity resolution working exactly as designed.

What is entity disambiguation?

Entity disambiguation is how AI decides which thing a name refers to when it could mean more than one. "Apple" the company versus the fruit. "Specularis" the agency versus the mineral.

AI resolves it by finding a well-defined entity — a distinct, consistently described identity that lots of independent sources agree on. Whoever has the clearest, best-corroborated identity wins the name.

And this isn't only a weird-brand-name problem. If your business name is generic, AI won't confuse you with something else — it just won't find a distinct you at all, and it'll name a competitor instead. Same root cause: no clear entity for AI to point at.

How do you fix it?

The fix isn't clever wording. It's giving AI a company identity so well-defined and so consistently corroborated that naming you becomes the obvious choice. This is the order that actually works:

  1. Tell AI directly, in code. Structured data (schema) on your site that names the entity, describes it specifically, and links out to your other profiles. It's the one lever you fully control, and it's free.
  2. Make every profile say the same thing. LinkedIn, Crunchbase, your X bio, any directory — identical name, founding year, location, description. Inconsistency makes AI hedge. Consistency makes it confident.
  3. Get named by independent sources. A couple of podcast appearances, a guest article, a press mention. AI trusts what others say about you far more than what you say about yourself.
  4. Claim a knowledge-graph entity. A Crunchbase entry, and eventually a Wikidata one. This is where AI literally looks up "what is X." A company node here is what finally separates you from the other meaning of your name.
  5. Publish the context. An article like this one — putting your name right next to what you actually do — teaches AI the association directly. Yes: this page is part of our own fix. You're reading the method work.

Where we are right now

We're running all five on ourselves, in the open, and posting the scoreboard every month. When we started, AI recommended us in zero of four target queries. We'd rather show you the climb from zero than pretend we were at the top the whole time.

That's the honest state: the mineral still shows up today. The entity we've built is new, and AI takes time to catch up as it re-reads the web. But the direction is the whole point — we're doing to our own name exactly what we do for clients, and you can watch it happen.
